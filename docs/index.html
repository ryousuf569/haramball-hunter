Ten attackers share one policy. They are up against eleven defenders sitting in a low block calibrated on StatsBomb tracking data. To win the episode they have to get the ball inside the dashed disc at (86, 34), radius 8 m, without letting mean attacker pitch control drop below 0.40. The orange is that pitch control: roughly, how much of each patch of grass the attacking side owns at that moment. It only exists over the attacking half, on a 31×34 grid, so the field stops near the halfway line instead of trailing off.

Worth saying plainly: this isn't trying to be football, and it isn't failing at it. Nobody shoots and nobody scores. An episode ends when the ball reaches that disc, when the defenders win it back, or when the clock runs out. All ten attackers share one policy, so they aren't ten players with positions and roles. The defenders are calibrated against real tracking data; the attackers were never asked to look like footballers. If the movement looks strange, that isn't the experiment going wrong. The only question being asked is whether the agent hits the behaviour rate I typed.

Req. is the number I typed: the fraction of attacker-ticks on which an attacker is allowed to be moving slower than 2.0 m/s. Ach. is what the trained policy actually did over 150,000 attacker-ticks, weights frozen. Leave it unconstrained and you get 0.885, which looks like a team standing around passing to each other.

Succ. is worth less than it looks. It comes from the same probe, which cuts episodes off at a fixed window and so counts short failures more often than long wins. The success numbers I actually trust are on the results page. Everything on this canvas is eight recorded episodes played back. No physics runs in your browser.
